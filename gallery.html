Popular Techniques

Digital art is not a single technique — it's a vast constellation of methods, each with its own workflow, community, and aesthetic identity. Understanding the most popular techniques helps you choose the right tools for your own creative goals.

Digital Painting

Digital painting mimics traditional painting techniques using software brushes. Artists use a stylus and drawing tablet (brands like Wacom or the Apple Pencil are popular) to apply strokes in apps like Procreate, Clip Studio Paint, or Adobe Photoshop. The result can range from photorealistic portraits to expressive, loose impressionist scenes. Many professional concept artists and illustrators use digital painting as their primary medium.

  • Tools: Procreate, Photoshop, Krita (free)
  • Hardware: Drawing tablet or iPad with stylus
  • Best for: Illustration, character art, fan art
  • Skill level: Beginner to advanced

Vector Illustration

Vector art uses mathematical paths instead of pixels, meaning artwork can be scaled to any size without losing quality. This makes it perfect for logos, icons, and graphic design. Adobe Illustrator and the free alternative Inkscape are the dominant tools. Vector illustration tends to have a clean, graphic look — bold lines and flat colors — though skilled artists push it into surprisingly painterly territories.

  • Tools: Adobe Illustrator, Inkscape, Affinity Designer
  • Hardware: Mouse or tablet
  • Best for: Logos, branding, flat-design illustration
  • Skill level: Beginner-friendly with a learning curve

3D Modeling & Rendering

3D art involves building three-dimensional objects in software, then lighting, texturing, and rendering them into final images or animations. Blender is the most popular free tool and has an incredibly active community. Professionals in film, gaming, and advertising use tools like Maya, Cinema 4D, and ZBrush. 3D art enables photorealistic scenes, stylized characters, product visualizations, and immersive environments.

  • Tools: Blender (free), Maya, Cinema 4D, ZBrush
  • Hardware: A powerful GPU helps significantly
  • Best for: Game assets, film VFX, product design, animation
  • Skill level: Moderate to advanced

Generative & Algorithmic Art

Generative art uses code and algorithms to create imagery, often with an element of randomness or mathematical systems. Artists write programs (in languages like Processing, p5.js, or GLSL shaders) that generate unique outputs each time they run. This technique sits at the intersection of art, mathematics, and programming. It has gained enormous popularity in the NFT space, where generative collections can produce thousands of unique artworks from a single algorithm.

  • Tools: p5.js, Processing, TouchDesigner, GLSL
  • Hardware: Any modern computer
  • Best for: Abstract art, NFTs, data visualization, live performance
  • Skill level: Requires basic coding knowledge
